## Aiuteur — Product Mission & Philosophy

### The Core User We're Building For
- The target is the **"middle 80%"** — creative people who sit between two extremes:
  - **Too casual:** Users who just want AI to generate everything for them (Manus, Google NotebookLM, etc.) — no creative control, generic output
  - **Too technical:** Power users who want granular control over every detail (Adobe Creative Suite users)
- Our user has **a story to tell** — jokes, ideas, short films, animations — but lacks the patience or technical depth to master professional tools
- They are **creative but not maximalist.** They care about the output having a human soul, not just being algorithmically produced

---

### What the Product Experience Should Feel Like
- **Fast-flowing** — users move through ideas quickly, never feeling stuck or bogged down
- **Flexible pacing** — users can speed through the parts they don't care about and slow down to get precise on the parts they do
- **Never friction-inducing** — there should never be a moment where the user wants to stop because it's too hard or too tedious
- Agentic features exist to **accelerate the low-care tasks**, not to replace the user's creative judgment
- High-control tools, canvases, and workspaces exist for **when the user wants to get specific** — not imposed on them by default

---

### What We Are Not
- **Not a slop generator.** We are not building UGC/TikTok ad factories that pump out 20 assets an hour with no human authorship
- **Not an agent takeover product.** If a feature deviates from user input, removes creative control, or makes decisions the user didn't sanction — it's out of scope
- **Not an Adobe replacement.** If a feature would only appeal to someone who wants to tweak every technical detail, it's not for our user
- Not trying to capture everyone — and that's intentional

---

### The North Star Test (for evaluating any feature)
Before adding, keeping, or shipping any feature, ask:
1. **Would the middle-80% user actually want this?** Or is it for the top 10% hyper-detail-oriented crowd?
2. **Does it add creative speed and flow?** Or does it introduce friction, confusion, or decision fatigue?
3. **Does it preserve human authorship?** Or does it let the agent overtake the user's creative intent?
4. **Does it focus the product?** Or does it add noise that obscures what Aiuteur actually does?

> *"We can't be all things for all people. Too many features — even if they all work well — create confusion and obfuscate what the product provides."*
